## LinkedIn Organic Content Strategy Playbook for B2B SaaS
# Step 1 — Define Your Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)
# Objective

Identify exactly who your content is intended to reach before creating a single post.

# Why it matters

Organic content is most effective when it addresses the needs of a clearly defined audience. Generic content attracts generic attention, while targeted content builds credibility with potential buyers.

# Actions
- Define your target industry.
- Define company size (startup, SMB, enterprise).
- Identify decision-makers (e.g., Founder, VP Marketing, RevOps Manager).
- List their top business challenges.
- Identify where they consume information and who influences their decisions.

# Expected Outcome
A documented ICP that guides every future content decision.

- Katelyn Bourgoin
- Justin Welsh


# Step 2 — Build a Credibility-First LinkedIn Profile
# Objective

Turn your LinkedIn profile into a landing page that immediately communicates expertise and trust.

# Why it matters

Before engaging with your content, potential buyers often visit your profile. A weak profile reduces the impact of even excellent content.

# Actions
- Use a professional profile photo.
- Write a headline focused on the audience you help.
- Explain your expertise in the About section.
- Include measurable results or case studies.
- Add a clear call-to-action.

# Expected Outcome
Visitors immediately understand:

- Who you help.
- What problems you solve.
- Why they should trust you.

# Sources
- Richard van der Blom
- Justin Welsh

# Step 3 — Define Your Content Pillars
# Objective

Create a repeatable content structure instead of publishing random ideas.

# Why it matters

Consistent positioning is easier to achieve when every post supports a small number of recurring themes.

# Actions
Choose 3–5 pillars, such as:

- Industry insights
- Customer pain points
- Product education
- Case studies
- Personal experiences
- Market trends
- Document each pillar with examples.

# Expected Outcome
Every future post fits within a clear strategic framework.

# Sources
- Justin Welsh
- Dave Gerhardt
- Julian Shapiro

# Step 4 — Create Content That Solves Real Buyer Problems
# Objective

Publish content that helps buyers make better decisions rather than simply attracting engagement.

# Why it matters

Decision-makers share useful content internally. Educational content builds authority and trust throughout the buying process.

# Actions

Create posts that:

- Answer frequently asked customer questions.
- Explain common mistakes.
- Share practical frameworks.
- Break down complex topics.
- Present customer lessons.
- Address different awareness levels.

# Expected Outcome
Content becomes a valuable educational resource rather than entertainment.

# Sources
- Amanda Natividad
- Justin Welsh
- Katelyn Bourgoin


# Step 5 — Publish Consistently
# Objective

Build familiarity through regular publishing.

# Why it matters

Organic growth compounds over time. Most creators stop before consistency produces meaningful results.

# Actions
- Publish consistently every week.
- Maintain quality standards.
- Review performance monthly.
- Continue publishing despite slow early growth.


# Expected Outcome
Steady audience growth and increasing brand recognition.

# Sources
- Justin Welsh
- Katelyn Bourgoin


# Step 6 — Repurpose Every High-Value Asset
# Objective

Maximize the return on every piece of content you create.

# Why it matters

Creating new content constantly is expensive. Repurposing increases reach without increasing production effort.

# Actions

Transform one long-form asset into multiple formats:

- LinkedIn posts
- Carousel posts
- Newsletter
- Short videos
- Blog articles
- Sales enablement material

# Expected Outcome
Higher content ROI with less production effort.

# Sources
- Ross Simmonds


# Step 7 — Build Authority Instead of Chasing Virality
# Objective

Become the trusted expert in your niche rather than pursuing maximum reach.

# Why it matters

Enterprise buyers prioritize expertise, credibility, and trust over popularity.

# Actions

Prioritize content that:

- Demonstrates expertise.
- Reduces buying risk.
- Shares evidence.
- Explains implementation.
- Educates decision-makers.
- Avoid optimizing exclusively for impressions or likes.

# Expected Outcome

Higher-quality audience and stronger brand positioning.

# Sources
- Richard van der Blom
- Julian Shapiro

  
# Step 8 — Engage Strategically
# Objective

Strengthen relationships through meaningful interactions.

# Why it matters

Comments and conversations often generate more qualified opportunities than broadcasting alone.

# Actions
- Respond thoughtfully to comments.
- Comment on industry leaders' posts.
- Start conversations with prospects.
- Answer questions publicly.
- Participate consistently in relevant discussions.

# Expected Outcome
Greater visibility among the right audience and stronger professional relationships.

# Sources
- Richard van der Blom
- Justin Welsh


# Step 9 — Measure Business Impact
# Objective

Evaluate success using business outcomes instead of vanity metrics.

# Why it matters

High engagement does not necessarily generate revenue.

# Actions

Track metrics such as:

- Qualified inbound leads.
- Demo requests.
- Sales conversations.
- Newsletter subscribers.
- Website traffic from LinkedIn.
- Pipeline influenced by organic content.

# Avoid relying solely on:

- Likes
- Impressions
- Follower count

# Expected Outcome
Content performance becomes aligned with business objectives.

# Sources
- Chris Walker
- Dave Gerhardt
- Richard van der Blom


# Step 10 — Continuously Improve the System
# Objective

Treat LinkedIn content as an evolving system rather than a series of isolated posts.

# Why it matters

Buyer behavior, algorithms, and business priorities change over time. Continuous refinement keeps the strategy effective.

# Actions
- Review top-performing posts every month.
- Identify recurring themes.
- Test different content formats.
- Update messaging based on customer feedback.
- Refresh content pillars quarterly.
- Document lessons learned.

# Expected Outcome
A scalable and continuously improving content engine.
