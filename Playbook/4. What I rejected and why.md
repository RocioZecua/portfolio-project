# What I Rejected and Why

## 1. Optimizing for Virality

Several content creators encourage marketers to maximize reach by creating highly engaging posts designed to generate impressions, comments, and follower growth. While this approach can increase visibility, I decided not to make virality a core recommendation in this playbook.

For B2B SaaS companies, the primary objective is not to become an influencer but to establish credibility with potential buyers. Enterprise purchasing decisions often involve multiple stakeholders, long sales cycles, and significant financial risk. In these situations, trust and expertise have a greater impact than broad exposure.

Richard van der Blom argues that "authority beats attention" and that relevance to the right audience is more valuable than reaching thousands of people who are unlikely to become customers. Based on this perspective, I believe marketers should prioritize building authority within their target market rather than chasing viral performance.

**Sources:**
- Richard van der Blom – "Authority Beats Attention"
- Justin Welsh (consistency and audience building)

## 2. Constantly Creating New Content Instead of Repurposing Existing Assets

Another idea I decided not to include is the assumption that every LinkedIn post should be created from scratch. While producing original content is important, I believe this approach is inefficient for most B2B SaaS teams, especially those with limited marketing resources.

Ross Simmonds demonstrates that a single high-quality asset—such as a webinar, podcast, or research report—can be transformed into multiple LinkedIn posts, newsletters, videos, and other formats. Repurposing allows companies to increase content output while maintaining quality and consistency.

For this reason, my playbook recommends treating content as a long-term asset rather than a one-time publication. Creating fewer but higher-quality pieces and systematically repurposing them is a more sustainable strategy than constantly producing new content.

**Source:**
- Ross Simmonds – "Content Distribution vs. Content Creation"

- ## 3. Making Every Post Story-Driven

Shaan Puri demonstrates how storytelling can capture attention and make ideas memorable. While I believe storytelling is a valuable communication tool, I decided not to recommend using it in every LinkedIn post.

Many B2B buyers visit LinkedIn to learn, solve problems, and evaluate potential vendors. In these situations, practical insights, industry expertise, and educational content often provide more value than personal stories alone.

Instead, I recommend using storytelling selectively—primarily to introduce complex ideas or make educational content more engaging—while ensuring that every post delivers actionable insights.

**Sources:**
- Shaan Puri – "Storytelling Masterclass"
- Julian Shapiro – Interview on authority-building through educational content
