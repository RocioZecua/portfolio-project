## After reviewing all ten experts, I noticed that everyone focuses on creating new content.

# I would add a "Buyer Question Library."


One idea I did not find in my research is to create a centralized "Buyer Question Library" that becomes the starting point for every LinkedIn content decision.

Instead of brainstorming topics from scratch, every question asked by prospects should be documented in a shared database. These questions can come from sales calls, product demos, customer support tickets, webinars, LinkedIn comments, emails, or conversations with existing customers.

Each question should be categorized by topic, buying stage, and frequency. The marketing team can then prioritize the questions that appear most often and transform them into LinkedIn posts, carousel documents, newsletters, videos, or long-form articles.

I believe this approach could work because it shifts content creation away from assumptions and toward real customer demand. Rather than guessing what the audience wants to read, companies would consistently create content that answers the exact questions buyers are already asking.

This system would also strengthen alignment between sales and marketing. Sales teams hear objections every day, while marketing teams create educational content. Connecting these two functions through a shared question library would make content more relevant, more useful, and more likely to support the buying process.

Although several experts emphasize understanding the audience, none of the sources I reviewed proposed building a structured repository of real buyer questions as the foundation of an organic LinkedIn content strategy. For that reason, I consider this to be an original addition to the playbook.
